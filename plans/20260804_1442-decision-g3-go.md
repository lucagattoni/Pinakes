## Why go, and why the drop runs did not decide the kind set

The probe says what it is, and it is worth quoting because it bounds every number above:

> **This is throwaway measurement code, not the G3 deriver.** […] A ceiling that is already too low
> is decisive; **a ceiling that is high proves only that the gate is not impossible.**

So the measurement settles **headroom** and nothing else. Using `--drop sibling` to remove a kind
from the design would be a ceiling gauge making a retrieval decision — the wrong instrument, and
one whose traversal is explicitly *"the probe's reading of APPROACH §3 and §4A"*, subordinate to
G5's implementation. Hence addition 1: the hypothesis is recorded and handed to the gate.

**The lower-bound argument holds.** `in-section` and `parent-child` derived **zero** edges on this
corpus, and no drop run ever raised reachability — so 9 is a **floor** for a corpus whose chunker
works, not a best case.

## The bound — read before citing any number here

**All 106 806 chunks have an empty `heading_path`.** RFC section numbering is not Markdown-shaped,
so `strategy = "structural"` degraded to size-based chunking in silence. Two consequences:

> **Corrected 20260807, and the decision is unaffected.** This record is left as written, but the
> cause above is the withdrawn one: no grammar failed to match, because none ran — `chunk.py`
> dispatched on **source type** and every type but `markdown` took the plain-text path. The bound
> below is unchanged, since it depends on the *absence* of heading paths rather than on why they
> were absent. **0.13.0 shipped `[chunking] headings = "numbered"`, but it is opt-in and this
> corpus's committed manifest does not set it** — so these numbers re-derive as written, and giving
> the corpus sections is a deliberate edit that makes it a different measurement.

* **Three of six kinds were never exercised.** `in-section` and `parent-child` derived nothing;
  `sibling` derived 106 506 edges that changed no outcome.
* **`sibling` here is not `sibling` in production.** With structural chunking dead, a "sibling" is
  an adjacent arbitrary *size-slice*, not an adjacent section. **Its zero is evidence about this
  graph, not a licence to drop the kind** — which is precisely why it goes to the gate as an arm
  rather than into the design as a deletion.

## Alternatives rejected

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Go, but exclude `sibling`** | Generalises from a corpus where the chunker silently failed, using an instrument that disclaims the authority to select kinds. The saving is real (99.2% of graph mass) but unproven — so it becomes a gate arm |
| **Fix `heading_path` first, re-measure** | The cheap half (a warning) yields no edges; the half that would means teaching the chunker RFC section numbering — corpus-specific scope creep. Re-opens a gate that passed. Real risk the corpus is never "good enough" |
| **Isolate the fan-out anomaly first** | **There is no anomaly.** It was inferred from a baseline read off the drop runs rather than measured; the measured baseline is monotonic. Withdrawn |

## A correction this decision rests on

An earlier reading of these runs claimed the with-authored baseline was *liftable 10, beyond 2*, and
that dropping `sibling` raised it to 12 — evidence that edge kinds compete for the fan-out budget
and that "more edges ⇒ more reachability" was false. **That baseline was inferred from the drop
runs, not measured.** The measured baseline is **12, beyond 0**, and every drop moves the count down
or not at all. The competition claim is withdrawn; the monotonicity it denied is what makes the
floor argument above valid.
